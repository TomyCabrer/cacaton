# Case 1 · Urda (28 October 2026)

A reconstruction for the drill, built from **preliminary press reports**. The investigation (CIAF, now AITAT) has not
published its findings and the cause is not established. Death toll in reports: 68 to 69; 200+ injured; about 544 on board.

## What was reported

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 08:31 | LZB cab-signalling transmission lost over about 63 km of the Madrid–Sevilla high-speed line. Trains braked to a stand. |
| after 08:31 | Iryo 6076 stops at the Urda entry signal E242 (between Mora and Urda, PK about 118). Its driver calls the control centre up to four times; the first calls go unanswered. |
| 08:36 | Control releases AVE 2080, following on the same track, to normal running. The AVE driver says he has a train ahead; the controller does not confirm the line is clear. |
| 08:41 | The Iryo is cleared to move but cannot reset LZB and cannot restart. |
| 08:48 | The Iryo reports it cannot restart. No action follows. |
| about 08:53 | The AVE hits the stationary Iryo (stopped about 14 minutes) at an estimated 160–200 km/h. |

The CIAF had earlier warned that after an LZB loss safety relied on the controller's visual check of the panel, and that
the centre had no alert for certain overruns.

## What the control centre could see

- **Speed:** no report says the centre's panel showed how fast a train was going.
- **Position:** after the LZB loss, following the trains "depended on the vigilance" of the staff, and the stopped
  Iryo is described as effectively invisible to the signalling once it could not restart its onboard systems. The
  transport minister rejected the claim that controllers lacked the tools to follow trains. So this point is disputed.
- The rule after an LZB loss was on-sight running at 40 km/h. The centre cleared the AVE for normal running.

## How the drill models it

- Both trains run at 200 km/h on track 2: **Train A** (AVE 2080, the pilot's seat) at PK 109.0, **Train B**
  (Iryo 6076) 1.9 km ahead. The clock starts at 08:30:56 and LZB fails four seconds later, at 08:31:00.
- From the failure on, the tower's panel shows only where each train was last seen. It never shows speed.
- Train B's driver brakes to a stand by himself and cannot restart. Train A has to be stopped by the tower, by voice.
  If nobody says anything, Train A reaches Train B in about a minute.
- Protocol **P-05 LZB failure** opens by itself from the alarm, in three steps: stop all trains; every train at a
  stand; continue on sight, 40 km/h.
- The pilot sees a standing train from 350 m. At 40 km/h that is enough to stop; at 200 km/h it is not.
- Copi writes to the tower only when something is going wrong (a train still moving, a release above 40, a train too
  fast after the stop, Train A running out of room behind Train B). It does not talk and does not explain.

Positions, speeds, the 350 m sight distance and the timings are the drill's, not data from the investigation.
